# GBP Post Topics — Framework Guide

Four-topic rotation system for Google Business Profile posts. One post per week, cycle repeats. Rotating topics prevents posting the same angle repeatedly, which GBP's algorithm treats as lower-effort content.

---

## Why 4 Topics?

Google's Map Pack ranking factors include posting frequency (weekly minimum) AND content diversity. A profile that posts the same "call us today" message every week signals low effort. A profile that rotates angles — speed, expertise, trust, range — signals active engagement.

Each topic also targets a different customer mindset:

| Topic | Customer mindset | They're thinking... |
|---|---|---|
| Speed / Emergency | Crisis | "I need this fixed TODAY" |
| Expertise / Brand | Considered | "I want someone who knows this brand" |
| Trust / Warranty | Cautious | "What if the repair doesn't last?" |
| Full Range | Uncertain | "Can they even fix my type of appliance?" |

---

## Topic 1: Emergency / Speed

**What it does:** Captures customers who have a broken appliance RIGHT NOW and are in search mode. This is your highest-intent audience — they're not researching, they're deciding.

**Core message:** We're fast. Same day. Available now.

**What to include:**
- Same-day or fast response claim (specific beats vague: "within hours" > "quickly")
- Service list (so they know you cover their appliance)
- One trust signal (warranty, license, or brand expertise)
- Phone CTA — not a link, a call

**Mistakes to avoid:**
- Don't open with your business name — open with the customer's problem
- Don't use "affordable" (vague and overused)
- Don't bury the phone number at the end of a long paragraph

**Tone:** Urgent, confident, direct. This is not a brand story — it's a dispatch signal.

---

## Topic 2: Brand / Expertise

**What it does:** Captures customers who have a specific brand (often premium: Sub-Zero, Wolf, Miele, Bosch) and are searching for someone qualified for it, not just a generic repair shop.

**Core message:** We specialize. We're trained. We use OEM parts.

**What to include:**
- Specific brand names (not "all major brands" — that's Topic 4)
- What makes your training or certification meaningful to the customer
- OEM parts mention (huge differentiator — fear of aftermarket parts is real)
- LEARN_MORE CTA pointing to a brands page

**Mistakes to avoid:**
- Don't claim certifications you don't have
- Don't list every brand — pick 4-6 and name them specifically
- Don't use the same brand list every week (rotate which brands lead)

**Tone:** Authoritative, specific, premium-adjacent. This is the post that signals expertise.

---

## Topic 3: Trust / Warranty

**What it does:** Addresses the #1 customer objection — "will this repair even last?" — before they have to voice it. Especially effective for customers who've had a bad repair experience before.

**Core message:** We stand behind our work. You're protected.

**What to include:**
- Warranty duration and what it covers (parts AND labor — both matter)
- What "we come back at no charge" actually means in practice
- Why your parts are better (OEM, not aftermarket)
- License + insurance mention (lowers perceived risk)
- BOOK CTA — this post targets customers who are close to deciding

**Mistakes to avoid:**
- Don't say "quality" or "best" — too generic
- Don't vague-promise "we'll make it right" without specifying what that means
- Don't include a phone number AND a booking link — pick one CTA

**Tone:** Calm, reassuring, specific. This is the post that closes decisions.

---

## Topic 4: Full Service Range

**What it does:** Reminds the entire local area that you service everything. Some customers assume repair shops specialize in one appliance type. This post casts the widest net.

**Core message:** Whatever you have, whatever brand — we fix it.

**What to include:**
- Full appliance list (bulleted for scannability)
- Full brand list (as many as honest to list)
- Service area (city + neighboring areas)
- Phone CTA — this is a broad awareness post, so CALL works better than BOOK

**Mistakes to avoid:**
- Don't open with your business name
- Don't be vague about which appliances — list them specifically
- Don't include marketing claims in this post — it's a reference post, keep it factual

**Tone:** Informational, comprehensive, inclusive. This is the post that catches everyone else.

## Post Format Best Practices

### Structure
```
[Hook — 1 sentence: the problem or the claim]

[Body — 2-4 sentences: what you offer and why it matters]

[Proof points — 2-3 bullets: specific, not generic]

[CTA — 1 line: what to do next]
```

### Character counts
- Standard posts: 1,500 character max — Google truncates beyond this
- Optimal length: 800-1,200 characters — enough to be useful, short enough to read on mobile
- Hook alone: 60-90 characters — must work as a standalone line in preview

### CTA types and when to use each

| CTA | Use for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `CALL` | Speed/Emergency and Full Range posts | Highest friction removal — one tap to call |
| `BOOK` | Trust/Warranty posts | Customer is close to deciding, send them to booking |
| `LEARN_MORE` | Expertise posts | Drive traffic to brands/services page |
| `SIGN_UP` | Promotions and offers | Captures emails; rare for home services |

### Emojis
- Use 2-3 max per post — one per key point
- Lead emoji signals tone: 🔧 = practical, 🏆 = premium, 🛡️ = trust, 🔩 = range
- Don't emoji every bullet point — it reduces the impact

## What Google Prioritizes

GBP posts contribute to profile prominence — one of the three Map Pack ranking factors (relevance, proximity, prominence). Specifically:

1. **Posting frequency** — weekly posts signal active business
2. **Post engagement** — clicks, calls from posts, direction requests
3. **Content freshness** — Google favors recently updated profiles over static ones

A profile that hasn't posted in 30 days looks like an inactive business to Google's algorithm.
